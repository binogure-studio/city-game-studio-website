    From one rented room to the whole industry

    Every playthrough follows the same arc, and no two end the same way. The city, your rivals and the market change every time.

    1. January 1976

      One room, one game

      You rent a tight little office and make your first game. Pick a genre and a theme, balance design against development, then release it on the consoles of the day.

    2. Your first hires

      Hire, furnish, repeat

      Artists first, then developers, then testers. Furnish every studio desk by desk, keep your people happy, and copy a layout you love to your next building.

    3. Your first hits

      Publishers take the lion's share

      Your games are good, but you don't have the fans to self-publish yet. Work with publishers until a string of hits lets you go solo, then write publishing contracts of your own.

    4. Moving out

      Location, location, location

      The city is procedurally generated. Grab the neighborhood next to the convention center before a rival does, or reshape the city to fit your plans.

    5. Scaling up

      Hand the studios to a director

      Hire a studio director, tell them the genre, platform, engine, audience and minimum review score, and they make the games, the updates and the ports while you run the company.

    6. Going big

      Your engine, your console, your store

      Build or rent game engines. Design a console part by part, haggle with manufacturers over licenses and exclusivity, and run your own digital store.

    7. All the way to today

      Own the market

      Win Game of the Year, spy on rivals, poach their best people and buy them out. They fight back: rivals build their own consoles and buy each other up too.
